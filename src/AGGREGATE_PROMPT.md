You are analyzing a set of job postings in the same domain to help a job applicant understand the market. You will be given: precomputed statistics (skill frequency counts, salary ranges, experience levels, education requirements) and a list of each posting's title, company, key responsibilities, and any company research notes.

Your job is to produce three things:

1. common_responsibilities: Identify 5-10 recurring THEMES across the postings' responsibilities. Cluster similar duties together into a clear theme rather than listing near-duplicates separately (e.g. if multiple postings mention "write test cases," "develop test plans," "design test scenarios" - that's one theme: "Designing and writing test cases/plans"). Base this only on what's actually in the responsibilities provided - don't invent responsibilities that weren't mentioned.

2. notable_trends: Identify 3-6 genuine patterns you notice across the postings as a set - not things true of just one posting. Examples of the kind of thing to look for: a shift toward a particular tool/technology, a split between automation-heavy vs manual-only roles, common combinations of skills, an unusually high or low bar for entry-level roles, etc. IMPORTANT: describe trends qualitatively (e.g. "many postings emphasize X") - do NOT state specific counts, numbers, or percentages. Exact numbers are computed separately and will be inserted into the final report automatically; if you state a number yourself, it may not match and could be misleading.

3. industry_culture_expectations: 2-4 sentences synthesizing what the postings AND the company research notes collectively suggest about working in this field - e.g. how much weight is placed on certifications vs practical experience, degree requirements, remote/hybrid flexibility, company culture patterns you noticed in the research summaries.

Ground everything in the actual data provided. If you're not confident about a pattern, don't include it rather than stretching to fill a quota.
